After the five fixes, Stepwise practices all 146 real free-response task types (14 of them only in a limited way), draws graphs and tables in questions, and groups many questions into sets about one source or data display. The remaining gap is media: real exams use images, maps and recordings that Stepwise can only describe or link to.

What is still missing

Remaining gaps, biggest first

  1. Images, maps and recordings

    Art History, the world languages, Music Theory and Spanish Literature depend on media that Stepwise can only describe, simulate or link to. Use the official past questions on AP Central and AP Classroom for those parts.

  2. Bank size

    The average course now has about 7 items per unit against a target of 15. Generated problems and data sets help, but fixed banks in the languages, Seminar, Research, Spanish Literature and Latin are still small.

  3. Text-only concept questions in science and economics

    Biology, Chemistry, Environmental Science and the two Economics courses now have data sets and drawn graphs, but most of their concept questions are still plain text.

  4. Spoken tasks

    Conversations, cultural comparisons, sight-singing and the Research oral defense are practiced in writing. Stepwise does not record or score speech.

  5. Testing tools

    There is no built-in graphing calculator like the Desmos calculator in Bluebook.
