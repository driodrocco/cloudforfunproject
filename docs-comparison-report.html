Close on content, behind on question style

Stepwise practices almost every unit and about half of the real free-response task types. The biggest difference is how questions are built: most real multiple-choice questions come in sets about a source, data table, graph, image or recording, while most Stepwise questions stand alone as text.

What would close the gap

Recommended fixes, biggest impact first

  1. Turn stand-alone questions into source and data sets

    For history, government, human geography, psychology, economics, biology, environmental science and statistics: groups of 3-4 questions about one document excerpt, data table or chart (drawn in the page). This is the single biggest style difference.

  2. Add the missing free-response types

    73 of the 146 real task types are missing. Quick wins: European History and African American Studies DBQs, US Gov quantitative analysis and SCOTUS comparison, Calculus graph and table questions, Statistics inference and investigative task, CS A 2D arrays, Physics 2 free response.

  3. Grow the banks to about 15 items per unit

    Most courses average around 5 per unit. The thinnest are Spanish Literature, Seminar, Research, Art History, Comparative Government and Human Geography.

  4. Draw graphs and diagrams in questions

    Calculus graphs of f', physics circuit and force diagrams, economics supply-and-demand and AD-AS graphs, and chemistry titration curves can all be drawn with code.

  5. Point to official audio and image practice

    For languages, Music Theory and Art History, link straight to the released exam questions on AP Central, since Stepwise cannot play audio or show artworks.

How the score works

Each course gets a similarity score out of 100, measured from Stepwise's actual content by a script (tools/compare.js):

  • Free-response types (35%): share of the real exam's free-response task types that Stepwise practices.
  • Unit coverage (20%): share of the course's units with at least one practice item.
  • Question style (25%): how well Stepwise matches the real multiple-choice style. For calculation exams, enough generated problem types (3 per unit); for source-based exams, the share of questions built on a passage; for code-based exams, the share built on code. Exams built on audio or images are capped at 50, and portfolio courses with no sit-down exam get 50.
  • Bank size (20%): total practice items compared with a target of 15 per unit.

Real exam formats come from the College Board course and exam descriptions and exam pages. AP Classroom features come from College Board's AP Classroom pages. This compares question types and structure, not the difficulty of individual questions. The real exam questions themselves are secure and not public.
